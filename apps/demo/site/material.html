The byte ledger

DESIGN §9 sanctioned a 3D renderer on exactly one page — this one — and attached a budget to it: ≤ 45 KB gz for the lazy chunk, with the rule that exceeding it deletes the page, not the budget. So it was built and measured rather than estimated. Both figures below are real builds of this repository's toolchain, tree-shaken and minified.

Lazy chunk What it draws gz Verdict
WebGLRenderer slice, refracting lozenge a physical material with transmission, one light 127.5 KB 2.8× over
the same slice, emptied to a plane a flat quad and nothing else 124.5 KB 2.7× over
assets/material-lens.js edge refraction at the rim, the one thing CSS cannot do < 1 KB shipped

The floor of a 3D library is the library, not the scene: emptying the scene to a single untextured quad saved 3 KB. There is no version of that page that fits, so the ruling applies itself and the shader is gone. What survives is the effect the ruling itself named as the only one a GPU genuinely buys — feTurbulence + feDisplacementMap at the rim — and it arrives through the same lazy-load discipline the renderer would have had to obey.

The gates

The upgrade is a dynamic import() and it is the last line of the loader — nothing is fetched on mount. All five have to hold: a stated motion preference outranks the design, the machine must have memory and cores to spare, the engine must support the effect, and the stage must be at least a quarter visible and the browser idle. Leaving the viewport tears it down again.

And in the product

None of this ships into a host page. The in-product “alive” moment is a six-line ambient ring that breathes while a copilot is working — rotation says wait, breathing says I'm here — and the hard ceiling for everything injected into somebody else's page is 40 KB gz, of which the whole glass appearance is a fraction.
